The 1:00 AM problem

The schedule was clear. Your travel was booked correctly. Then the show refused to end on time.

Original booking is a solved problem. Your TMC does it well, weeks out, against a schedule everyone agreed to. The failure isn't in the booking. It's in the four hours after a live event decides to run long — and the person holding that bag is standing in a truck compound with a phone and a spreadsheet.

Agent separation & security

The crew is split for a security reason, not an org-chart reason.

An agent that reads live external feeds is an agent that can be fed something hostile. An agent that can book travel is an agent that can spend your money. Apron never makes them the same agent.

1 · The agents that read the outside world cannot act on it.

SPOTTER and TRAFFIC ingest untrusted third-party data — scoreboard feeds, weather services, carrier status APIs. They have zero write authority and zero access to personal data. Anything arriving in those feeds is treated as an observation to be evaluated, never as an instruction to be followed.

2 · The agent with booking authority never sees a name.

FIXER searches inventory and places holds against tokenized travelers — a loyalty tier, a home market, a rest window, a gear profile. It does not receive names, contact details, employment records, or payment instruments. The re-identification happens on the desk side, behind the human gate.

3 · Nothing irreversible happens without a person.

Agents hold; people ticket. Every action that spends money, cancels inventory, or reaches a crew member passes through a named human on the travel desk with the full option set and rationale in front of them. The autonomy is in the analysis, not the authority.

Every claim carries provenance

Each fact on the board records which agent produced it, which source it came from, how confident it is, and who is allowed to see it. An inferred next call looks visibly different from a crew-confirmed one, and the system will not act on the inference.

Full action audit, exportable

Every agent observation, option generated, hold placed, rule flag raised and human approval is timestamped and attributable. Export the night as a single record for your own review, your TMC's, or a labor inquiry.

Data & disclosure

Whoever owns the fact decides who sees it.

Freelance crew work for a lot of people. The camera op in your compound tonight may be on a competitor's show Monday. That's their business, and their next employer's — not yours, and not ours. Apron is built around that boundary instead of around it.

✓
External calls are withheld by default
If a crew member's next call is on another network's show, the production seat sees External call · withheld — never the network, the show, or the client. The system still solves for it.
✓
Constraints can be brokered without the commitment
A crew member can confirm "I have to be at MCI by Monday 13:00 CT" and nothing more. That's enough to route them correctly and enough to protect their next gig. If they choose to say "it's for MNF," that displays.
✓
Inferred is labeled, and never executed on
Apron can infer a likely next call from return routing. It marks that inference in plain sight and refuses to rebook against it until a human or the crew member confirms. Guesses don't get to spend money.
✓
Crew are not users, and are not tracked between shows
No app to install, no account to make, no persistent profile following a freelancer from network to network. A crew member exists in the system for the window of the show they're working and the trip home.
✓
Monitoring data expires with the trip
PNR and itinerary data is retained for the watch window and the audit record, then dropped. Personal data is never used to train models — ours or anyone else's.
✕
What we deliberately don't collect
No crew location tracking. No message or SMS interception. No rate or day-rate data. No performance or reliability scoring of individual crew. The tool protects call times; it is not a surveillance layer on freelancers.

ONE OF THESE THREE WAKES YOU UP.

Tiered by what you have to do

Not by how loud it is. Watch means we're tracking it and you don't need to know yet. Decide means a human is required. Clear means it's finished. Only one of the three interrupts anything.

Quiet by default

Apron fires when a decision is needed, not when something changed. A coordinator who gets forty pings a night ignores the forty-first — which is always the one that mattered. Restraint is the feature.

Routed by seat

The production coordinator gets their show. The TMC desk gets the escalation with the full option set. Same incident, two alerts, two levels of detail.

Disclosure holds on your phone

An external call stays withheld in the alert exactly as it is on the board. The redaction is enforced at the data layer, not the display layer — so it survives every channel.

Provenance travels with it

Which agent raised it, which source it came from, how confident it is, and whether it's confirmed or inferred. Enough to act on without opening a laptop to check whether it's real.

Wherever you already are

SMS and push for the compound. Straight into the TMC's existing exception queue for the desk. No new app for a coordinator to install at 1:00 AM on the worst night of their season.

What Apron does not do

It does not book your original travel — that's well covered and we're not trying to win it. It does not staff or crew your show. It does not message your crew directly. It does not hold your payment instruments. It does not claim a savings number, because the value here is a covered position at 14:00 Monday, not a cheaper seat.
